    Getting Warmed Up and in the Right Mindset.

    1.

    A \(13\text{ft}\) ladder rests against a vertical wall. If the bottom of the ladder slides away at a rate of \(3\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}\text{,}\) how fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall when the top of the ladder is \(5\text{ft}\) from the ground?
    🔗
    (a)
    Write down all known information (with units), identify the unknown(s), and draw a picture (given below).
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(x=?\text{ft}\text{,}\) \(y=5\text{ft}\text{,}\) \(z=13\text{ft}\text{,}\) \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=3\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}\text{,}\) \(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=?\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}\text{,}\) \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}=0\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Write equations to relate the variables to each other.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(x^2+y^2=z^2\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Take derivatives, using the chain rule, to relate the rates to each other.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=2z\dfrac{dz}{dt}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Plug in numbers and solve for the unknown.
    🔗
    Hint.
    You didn’t know \(x\) in the part (a), but do you know it now?
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(2\left(\sqrt{13^2-5^2}\right)(3)+2(5)\dfrac{dy}{dt}=2(13)(0) \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{36}{5}\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
