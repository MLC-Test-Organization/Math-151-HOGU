    Let Us Get Our Hands On.

    1.

    Consider vectors \(\mathbf{a}=-4\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{b}=2\mathbf{i}-5\mathbf{j}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    Find the scalar, or dot, product of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}=-23\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Find the scalar projection of \(\mathbf{a}\) onto \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) \(\text{comp}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\text{comp}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a})=\dfrac{-23\sqrt{29}}{29}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Find the orthogonal, or vector, projection of \(\mathbf{a}\) onto \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) \(\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\text{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{a})=\left\langle \dfrac{-46}{29}, \dfrac{115}{29}\right\rangle\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Find the angle between \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) \(\theta\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-23\sqrt{29}}{145}\right)\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Eliminate the parameter \(t\) in each of the following parametric functions and simplify to Cartesian form involving \(x\) and \(y\) only.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=1-t\text{,}\) \(x=3t-2\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{3}x\) or \(x=1-3y\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(y=4t+1\text{,}\) \(x=t^2-5\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(x=\dfrac{1}{16}y^2-\dfrac{1}{8}y-\dfrac{79}{16}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(y=\sqrt{t}+8\text{,}\) \(x=7-2t\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y=\sqrt{\dfrac{7-x}{2}}+8\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=1-\sin(t)\text{,}\) \(x=1+\cos(t)\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((1-y)^2+(x-1)^2=1\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(y=2\cos(t)+2\text{,}\) \(x=-1+3\sin(t)\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\left(\dfrac{x+1}{3}\right)^2+\left(\dfrac{y-2}{2}\right)^2=1\)

    4.

    The work \(\text{W}\) (in Joules (\(\text{J}\))) done by a force \(\mathbf{F}\) with a displacement \(\mathbf{d}\) is given by the formula \(\text{W}=\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{d}\) or \(\text{W}=|\mathbf{F}||\mathbf{d}|\cos(\theta)\) where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf{F}\) and \(\mathbf{d}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    Find the work done by the force \(\mathbf{F}=6\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\) with a displacement vector \(\mathbf{d}=2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(14\text{ J}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Find the work done by the force \(\mathbf{F}=6\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\) with a displacement vector \(\mathbf{d}=3\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(20\text{ J}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Find the work done by the force \(\mathbf{F}=6\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j}\) with a displacement vector \(\mathbf{d}=-\mathbf{i}+3\mathbf{j}\text{.}\) What else can you say about the vectors \(\mathbf{F}\) and \(\mathbf{d}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(0\text{ J}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{F}\) and \(\mathbf{d}\) are orthogonal
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Find the work done by the force \(\mathbf{F}\) with a displacement vector \(\mathbf{d}\) if \(|\mathbf{F}|=8\text{,}\) \(|\mathbf{d}|=6\text{,}\) and \(\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(24\sqrt{2}\text{ J}\)

    5.

    Suppose that a box has been pulled \(15\text{ m}\) up a \(30^\circ\) incline by a rope with a force \(\mathbf{F}\) with a magnitude \(|\mathbf{F}|=100\text{ N}\text{.}\) Note that the reference angle for work problems is always taken from the direction of motion.
    🔗
    (a)
    Suppose that the force is applied parallel to the direction of motion, as shown in the figure below.
    
    A force applied to a box on a ramp, parallel to the direction of motion.
    🔗
    How much work does the rope do on the box?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(1500\text{ J}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Suppose that the force is applied \(30^\circ\) counterclockwise from the direction of motion, as shown in the figure below.
    
    A force applied to a box on a ramp, at an angle \(30^\circ\) counterclockwise from the direction of motion.
    🔗
    How much work does the rope do on the box? How does this compare with your answer from part (a)?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(750\sqrt{3}\text{ J}\text{,}\) it is less since the component of the applied force in the direction of motion is less
