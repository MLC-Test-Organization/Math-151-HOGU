    Let Us Get Our Hands On.

    1.

    If we are given an \(x\)-value \(x=a\) and a function \(f(x)\) which is differentiable at \(x=a\text{,}\) the the linearization \(L(x)\) represents the linear approximation of \(f(x)\) close to \(x=a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    Find the linearization of \(f(x)=\sqrt{x}\) at \(a=4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(L(x)=\dfrac{1}{4}(x-4)+2\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Approximate \(\sqrt{3.95}\) using linearization.
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(\sqrt{3.95} = f(3.95) \approx L(3.95)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f(3.95) \approx L(3.95) = 1.9875\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Find the linearization of \(g(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}\) at \(a=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(L(x)=g(a)+g'(a)(x-a)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(L(x)=-\dfrac{1}{4}(x-2)+\dfrac{1}{2}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Approximate \(\dfrac{1}{2.1}\) using linearization.
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(\dfrac{1}{2.1} = f(2.1) \approx L(2.1)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(g(2.1) \approx L(2.1) = 0.475\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    The radius of a sphere was measured to be \(5\text{ mm}\) with a maximum error in measurement of \(0.06\text{ mm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    Use differentials to estimate the maximum error in the calculated surface area of the sphere.
    🔗
    Hint 1.
    \(\text{Surface Area} = S = 4\pi r^2\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 2.
    \(\Delta S \approx dS = S'(a) dr\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(dS = 2.4\pi\text{ mm}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    What is the relative error in measuring the surface area of the sphere?
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(\text{Relative error} = \dfrac{dS}{S}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\dfrac{dS}{S} = 0.024\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    What is the percentage error in measuring the surface area of the sphere?
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(\text{Percentage error} = \text{Relative error}\times 100\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\text{Percentage error } = 2.4%\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Find the critical number(s) and relative/local extrema (maxima and minima) of each of the following functions. Be careful to check whether the derivative changes sign by using a sign chart!
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{3}x^3-\dfrac{5}{2}x^2+6x-3\)
    🔗
    Answer.

    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    \(g(t)=-3t^{\frac{1}{3}}(4-t)^{\frac{2}{3}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Critical numbers at \(t=0,\dfrac{4}{3},4\text{;}\) Relative minimum at \(\left(\dfrac{4}{3},g\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right)\right)\text{;}\) Relative maximum at \((4,g(4))\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(h(\theta)=\theta e^{-\sqrt[3]{\theta}}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Critical numbers at \(\theta=0,27\text{;}\) No relative minimum; Relative maximum at \((27,h(27))\)

    5.

    What is/are the value(s) of \(c\) that satisfy the conclusion of Rolle’s Theorem for the given functions on the given intervals? If Rolle’s Theorem does not apply, state which condition(s) it fails.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(x)=x^2-4x+3\) on \([1,3]\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(c=2\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(g(x)=\dfrac{1}{x^2}\) on \([-1,1]\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Since \(g(x)\) is not continuous or differentiable at \(x=0\text{,}\) \(g(x)\) is not continuous for all \(x\) in \([-1,1]\text{,}\) nor differentiable for all \(x\) in \((-1,1)\text{,}\) and the Rolle’s Theorem does not apply.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(h(x)=\cos(x)\) on \([0,2\pi]\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(c=\pi\)
