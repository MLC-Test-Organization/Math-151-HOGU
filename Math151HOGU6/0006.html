    1.

    Recall the general derivative for logarithmic functions:
    \begin{equation*} \dfrac{d}{dx}\log_b(f(x))=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}\dfrac{1}{\ln(b)}f'(x)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Use the chain rule to find the derivatives of the following functions. Note that you may have to apply the chain rule more than once.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(x) = \log(\sqrt{x})\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\ln(10)}\dfrac{1}{x}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(g(x) = (\ln(3x+1))^4\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(g'(x) = \dfrac{12(\ln(3x+1))^3}{3x+1}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(h(x)=\dfrac{(1-5x^3)^4}{\log_2(5x)}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(h'(x)=\dfrac{-60x^2(1-5x^3)^3\log_2(5x)-\frac{(1-5x^3)^4}{x\ln(2)}}{(\log_2(5x))^2}\)

    2.

    Find the derivatives of the following functions using logarithmic differentiation.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(x)=x^{\sin(2x)}\)
    🔗
    Hint 1.
    \(\ln(f(x))=\ln\left(x^{\sin(2x)}\right)=\sin(2x)\ln(x)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 2.
    \(\dfrac{1}{f(x)}f'(x)=2\cos(2x)\ln(x)+\dfrac{\sin(2x)}{x}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(f'(x)=x^{\sin(2x)}\left(2\cos(2x)\ln(x)+\dfrac{\sin(2x)}{x}\right)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(g(x)=\cos^{\tan(x)}(x)\)
    🔗
    Hint 1.
    \(\ln(g(x))=\ln\left(\cos^{\tan(x)}(x)\right)=\tan(x)\ln(\cos(x))\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 2.
    \(\dfrac{1}{g(x)}g'(x)=\sec^2(x)\ln(\cos(x))+\tan(x)\dfrac{1}{\cos(x)}(-\sin(x))\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(g'(x)=\cos^{\tan(x)}(x)\left(\sec^2(x)\ln(\cos(x))-\tan^2(x)\right)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(h(x)=\dfrac{(2x+7)^7(-x-6)^3}{(-3x+4)^4(-8x-9)^{10}}\)
    🔗
    Hint 1.
    \(\ln(h(x))=\ln\left(\dfrac{(2x+7)^7(-x-6)^3}{(-3x+4)^4(-8x-9)^{10}}\right) = 7\ln(2x+7)+3\ln(-x-6)-4\ln(-3x+4)-10\ln(-8x-9)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Hint 2.
    \(\dfrac{1}{h(x)}h'(x)=\dfrac{7}{2x+7}(2)+\dfrac{3}{-x-6}(-1)-\dfrac{4}{-3x+4}(-3)-\dfrac{10}{-8x-9}(-8)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(h'(x)=\dfrac{(2x+7)^7(-x-6)^3}{(-3x+4)^4(-8x-9)^{10}}\left(\dfrac{14}{2x+7}-\dfrac{3}{-x-6}+\dfrac{12}{-3x+4}+\dfrac{80}{-8x-9}\right)\)

    7.

    The growth of a population of bacteria is often modeled exponentially with the equation
    \begin{equation*} y(t)=y(0)e^{kt}\text{,} \end{equation*}
    where \(y(0)\) represents the initial population, \(k\) is the relative growth rate, and \(t\) represent time. You may leave answers to the following questions in mathematical symbols, but be sure to include units!
    🔗
    (a)
    Suppose that a population of bacteria initially contains \(3\) million cells and grows exponentially at a relative growth rate of \(40\%\) per hour. Find an expression for the number of bacteria present after \(t\) hours.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y(t)=3e^{0.4t}\text{million cells}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    At what time will the population of bacteria reach \(6\text{million cells}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(t = \dfrac{\ln(2)}{0.4}\text{hours}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    What is the instantaneous rate of growth of the bacteria present after \(6\) hours?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(y'(6)=1.2e^{2.4}\dfrac{\text{million cells}}{\text{hour}}\)

    8.

    Radiocarbon dating is used to determine the age or organic matter by measuring the amount of radioactive Carbon-\(14\) in the material compared to the non-radioative Carbon-\(12\) in the material. The half life of Carbon-\(14\) is \(5730\) years, and we model the decay of Carbon-\(14\) into Carbon-\(12\) with the equation
    \begin{equation*} y(t)=y(0)e^{kt}\text{,} \end{equation*}
    where \(y(0)\) represents the initial amount of Carbon-\(14\) in the material, \(k\) is the relative decay rate, and \(t\) represents time. You may leave your answers to the following questions in mathematical symbols, but be sure to include units!
    🔗
    (a)
    What is the relative decay rate for Carbon-\(14\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(y(5730)=\dfrac{1}{2}y(0)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(k=\dfrac{-\ln(2)}{5730}\text{year}^{-1}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Find an expression for the proportion of Carbon-\(14\) remaining in the material after \(t\) years.
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(p(t)=\dfrac{y(t)}{y(0)}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(p(t)=e^{\frac{-\ln(2)}{5730}t}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    What proportion of Carbon-\(14\) remains in the material after \(2000\) years?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(p(2000)=e^{\frac{-\ln(2)}{5730}(2000)}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Find the instantaneous rate of decay of the proportion of Carbon-\(14\) in the material after \(2000\) years.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(p'(2000)=\dfrac{-\ln(2)}{5730}e^{\frac{-\ln(2)}{5730}(2000)}\text{year}^{-1}\)

    9.

    We often model change in temperature using Newton’s Law of Cooling with the equation
    \begin{equation*} T(t)=T_s+(T(0)-T_s)e^{kt}\text{,} \end{equation*}
    where \(T(0)\) is represents the initial temperature of the object, \(T_s\) represents the temperature of the surroundings, \(k\) is the relative rate of change, and \(t\) represents time. Suppose that a cup of recently brewed coffee is currently too hot to drink, being \(200^\circ\text{F}\text{,}\) and that the temperature of the room is \(70^\circ\text{F}\text{.}\) After half an hour, the coffee is measured to be at a temperature of \(80^\circ\text{F}\text{.}\) You may leave answers to the following questions in mathematical symbols, but be sure to include units!
    🔗
    (a)
    Find an expression for the temperature, in degrees Fahrenheit, of the coffee in the cup after \(t\) minutes.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Write down all of your known information. Solve for the constants in the model that you don’t know.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(T(t)=70+130e^{\frac{-\ln(13)}{30}t}{^\circ\text{F}}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    How long after brewing the coffee will it be ready to drink, at a temperature of \(120^\circ\text{F}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(t=\dfrac{-30\ln\left(\frac{5}{13}\right)}{\ln(13)}\text{min}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    What is the instantaneous rate of change of the temperature of the coffee after \(2\) minutes?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(T'(2)=\dfrac{-13\ln(13)}{3}e^{\frac{-\ln(13)}{15}}{^\circ\text{F}}\)
